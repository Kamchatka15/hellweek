## 1. What the test was actually testing

The prompt says it plainly: can the factory take 99 Nights' *machine* and make a different game from it. The honest answer after one session:

- **The design side worked as advertised.** The packet arrived in the factory's own shape (six markers found by `intake_zip.py`), so nothing was re-derived. The brief, the cut line, the theme, the wedge and the lighting numbers went straight to disk. The only things I did to Justin's design were the two checks the skill says run on his typing too (platform blocks: none real; budget: inside it as drawn).
- **The engine side did not.** `docs/GAME_FACTORY.md` promised "a new game adds files under `games/<slug>/` and changes nothing in `src/core/`". That was true for a second *sweep-and-bank* game. It was false for a survive game, and it was false at the first line of the loader: `ContentLoader.validate` demanded a flood cooldown, upgrade roles, an offline cap, and a payout zone. The engine was one-loop-shaped, and nobody could see it until a differently-shaped title arrived. Filed as E-0007.

So the learning is not "the factory failed"; it is that **a factory with one title in it has an engine that is a generalisation of that title**. The second title is the first real test of the word "generic", and it costs one attended session of engine work. The third title is the test of whether the slots are now right.

### 4.14 The desert, and the obelisk as a workbench — the big one
Justin: build world 1 as a desert the size of a 99 Nights biome, with rocks and trees and cactus, cactus converts to water, and the obelisk is the workbench.

**Engine.** Two new generic modules. `SackService` — a carry that holds typed items instead of a number, because "two cactus and an ashwood" is not the same thing as "three units" and nothing downstream works without that distinction. `RecipeService` — a bag in, products out, sweeping the recipe list repeatedly so a later recipe can eat what an earlier one made. Recipes run in pack order, deliberately: leaving the order to a sort would make a pack's behaviour depend on a comparison function nobody can read.

**The biome.** `tools/gen_desert.py` emits 1,156 scenery parts from a fixed seed — dunes as mostly-buried spheres so only a crest shows, mesas as shrinking slabs, dead trees as a tapered trunk plus angled limbs, plus scrub, ruins, bone scatter and one dry wash for the eye to follow. A 700-stud disk, four times the old area. Harvest nodes (39 cactus, 32 boulders, 54 deadfall) are placed by the loop so they respawn at dawn.

**Water is an ingredient, not a meter.** A desert invites a thirst bar; the brief already parked that as a fourth HUD number that punishes existing and teaches nothing. Water as the thing every recipe needs does the opposite job — it makes the second and third resources matter and it gives the obelisk a reason to be a bench. The Wardstone (Water + 2 Stone) lifts a Weight, which ties the workbench directly to the Gift and makes that a three-way choice.

**Two limits found the hard way.** Studio refuses a script Source at 200,000 characters and the generated desert was 314KB — the generator now emits short aliases and splits into chunks that an `init.luau` stitches back. And my first end-to-end test never fed the fire, so night one ended the run and reset the bowl before dawn ever arrived; the mechanic was fine and the test was wrong. Worth remembering: in a game where the run resets, a test that skips the survival part is testing a different game.

### 4.15 Two bugs the desert exposed, and a lighting rule that does not scale
**The recipe resolver handed back what it had eaten.** Offering two cactus, two ashwood and two stone returned an Emberwood *and* the Water that made it. The split between "product" and "leftover" was reading a produced-ever tally instead of the pool, and an intermediate that a later recipe consumed is gone however many times it was made. The pool is the only truth. Fixed, then checked against six bags by hand: `4 cactus + 2 ashwood + 2 stone` cascades water → emberwood → heartwood in one dawn, `3 ashwood` alone comes back untouched.

**Recipe order is a design decision, not a detail.** With a greedy resolver the first recipe that fits eats the water, and Heartwood was listed above Wardstone — so a player with cactus, wood and stone got the deep fuel tier and never the item that answers the Gift. Wardstone now outranks it. The config says why, because the next person to reorder that list will change the game without meaning to.

**The lighting numbers did not survive the world getting four times bigger.** The rig tuned on a 350-stud shore left a 700-stud desert as a black void with a lit island in it — the same "darkness at first is worthless" failure, reappearing because the fix had been a set of numbers rather than a rule. The rule, now written into `world.luau`: a player sixty studs out must be able to see the sand and the cactus around them, while the obelisk is still obviously the brightest thing in the world. And a bald corridor was cut along the spawn line, because the generator kept standing a dead tree between the player and the one thing the first minute has to show them.

### 4.16 Four worlds, the fifth point, and an obelisk worth looking at
Four corrections in one pass, and the first is the one worth keeping.

**The point triangles were inverted.** They filled the star's points apex-inward at the obelisk — the opposite of what a point means — and I had written the maths correctly and trusted it. The error was in the WedgePart: which corner holds the right angle is a convention, I assumed one, and never looked at the result from above. Rebuilt as a fan of tapering strips, which cannot be got backwards, and verified with a top-down capture. **A shape built from an assumed convention needs one look, not one derivation.**

**Four worlds, not five**, with point 5 carrying the offering table. Dropping The Long Salt was the honest cut: once the desert took slot one, both were open arid ground. The table replacing the standalone bowl also made the pentagram carry the whole game — four futures and the place you trade for them — and forced the feed zone down from the whole 30-stud stone to a 10-stud circle at the obelisk's foot, because standing on a point must not also feed the fire. That number then had to move again: the stepped base is 13.4 wide, so a player stopped against it stands at 8.7 from the centre and an 8-stud zone is one they cannot reach.

**The whole world is lit now**, which is a different instruction from the last lighting pass. Contrast is now carried by the obelisk being the only warm light and the only thing that gets brighter, rather than by taking light away from everything else.

**The obelisk was genuinely low-resolution** — seven stacked boxes, a boxy cap, glyphs made of floating spheres — and `tools/gen_obelisk.py` replaces the Blender translation with 52 shaft slices, a real pyramid cap and gold marks recessed into channels that follow the taper. Skulls came off: at this scale under a warm wash they were smudges. The stone then read as terracotta, because warm light on grey stone always will; the wash went near-neutral and only the flame and the sand pool are allowed to be warm.

### 4.17 Day and night as two rigs, and a bright world that is still wrong
Justin: the desert should be brighter than the camp. The fix that mattered was structural rather than numeric — `setMood` had been tweening two properties (Brightness, one Atmosphere value) off a single rig, which can only ever produce "the same place, dimmer". `ContentLoader.tweenLighting` now moves the whole rig, and the pack carries two: a bright bleached day and a cold night. That is what lets a world look like a *different place* after dark.

**Bright is easy; bright and WRONG took a second pass.** The first version was a pleasant blue-sky beach. Roblox gives you postcard blue for free and saturation alone will not take it away. Heavy haze (5) on a bone-coloured Atmosphere bleaches the sky toward the ground, a low sun (7.1) gives hard long shadows instead of midday flatness, and the register came back.

**The gradient the owner asked for falls out of the parts, not a trick:** the pentagram slab is dark slate, the obelisk is charcoal, and its warm lights drop to 12% in daylight — so the camp reads darker than the waste by day and is the only warm thing in it by night, without either being faked.

### 4.18 The opening act, flipped
The English became the voice — biggest on screen, blood red, horror face, drips — and the transliteration dropped beneath it at a third the size to work as **aura** rather than as information. The register system in `OpeningAct` is now narration / voice / echo, which is the honest description of what those three things do.

Corrected the owner's line from "You must of fell asleep" to "You must've fallen asleep" and said so in the brief rather than changing it quietly: it is the first sentence a stranger reads, and in shipped narration the original reads as a typo instead of as a voice. One line to revert.

### 4.19 The 360 swap confirmed, chests, and held things that do not kill
**Confirmed and wired.** The world is now two layers — fixtures that never change (obelisk, stone, points, table, light) and biomes that swap whole (scenery, ground, day rig, night rig, fog). Mastering a world rebuilds 1,400 parts of desert into 2,100 parts of dead forest around a player who has not moved. All four worlds are generated from one parameterised generator: the shapes are shared, the palette, density and dominant silhouette change.

**Teepees and chests.** A cone of leaning poles with a hide skin, open toward the obelisk, a chest in the mouth. The scenery says where a teepee IS; the loop decides what is in the chest and when it restocks, so the world does not have to be rebuilt to refill it. A chest that overflows a full sack spills the rest on the ground rather than eating it.

**"Weapons to defend himself" answered with wards, not kills**, and flagged rather than assumed. The settled answer is that the Tempter is unkillable and that is not squeamishness — in this genre the threat being unkillable IS the game, and a weapon that ends it ends the loop with it. So: a Brand holds it 9 studs off and carries light; the **Sigil**, the artifact of faith, holds it 19. A ward is a second pane of glass that travels with the player. It buys the distance to walk home. The owner has been told plainly that a real kill mechanic is available and is a different night.

**Greedy order bit twice in one session.** Wardstone above Heartwood earlier, and then tool recipes below item recipes — which meant a *generous* offering made emberwood out of every stick and never a tool, while a stingy one got the knife. Tools now come first with `limit = 1`, and the loop drops a tool recipe once everybody holds it so it stops quietly eating a stone every dawn. With a greedy resolver, list order IS the design.
